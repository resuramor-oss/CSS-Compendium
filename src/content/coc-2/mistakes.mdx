### Cabling mistakes

- **Using the wrong pin-out.** T568A and T568B are both valid, but both ends of a straight-through cable must match.
- **Untwisting too much wire.** Keep the twists as close to the connector as possible to reduce crosstalk.
- **Not inserting wires fully.** The copper should reach the end of the RJ45 connector before crimping.
- **Damaging the conductors while stripping.** Nicked copper weakens the cable and can cause intermittent faults.
- **Skipping the cable test.** Always test both ends and record the result.
- **Running data cable next to mains power.** Interference and safety hazards result; use separation and proper raceway.
- **Exceeding 100 meters.** Copper Ethernet runs beyond the limit may link at reduced speed or fail.

### Addressing and configuration mistakes

- **Duplicate IP addresses.** Check the DHCP scope and static assignments.
- **Wrong subnet mask or gateway.** The client may reach local devices but not other networks.
- **Wrong DNS server.** The client can ping an IP but cannot resolve names.
- **DHCP pool exhaustion or overlap.** Confirm the pool size and that it does not overlap static addresses.
- **Leaving default router credentials.** Change the administrator password before deployment.
- **Using WEP or an open network.** Use WPA2/WPA3 with a strong passphrase.
- **Disabling the firewall unnecessarily.** Open only the ports that are required.
- **Forgetting to save the router configuration.** Reboot the device and verify that settings persist.

### Testing and documentation mistakes

- **Testing only one client.** Check wired and wireless clients, local resources, and internet access.
- **Ignoring intermittent faults.** Re-test after moving cables and devices; a link light is not proof of a good cable.
- **No labels.** Unlabelled cables make maintenance difficult and cost marks.
- **No network diagram or IP sheet.** Document the topology, addresses, and device settings.
- **Leaving waste behind.** Follow 5S and WEEE disposal requirements.
